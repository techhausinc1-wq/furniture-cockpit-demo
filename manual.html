Lead Finder Manual

A complete walkthrough of what this tool does and how to use every part of it — written for anyone, whether or not you've touched it before.

What this tool actually is

Modern Line Furniture makes custom seating, tables, booths, and lounge furniture for restaurants, hotels, and hospitality spaces. To grow the business, someone needs to find companies who might want to sell or represent Modern Line's furniture — sales reps who could add it to their line card, dealers who could stock it, wholesalers who could distribute it, or even other furniture manufacturers who'd want to partner up. Finding those companies used to mean manually searching the internet, checking if a company already had a competing line, tracking down someone's actual name and phone number, and writing all of it down somewhere so it didn't get lost or duplicated.

This tool does that searching for you. It goes out and finds real, currently-operating companies that fit what Modern Line is looking for, checks whether you've already found that company before, and saves everything about it — the company name, their website, why they're a good fit, and a real contact if one is publicly listed — in one shared list that everyone on the team can see and work from.

It also handles what happens after you find a lead: assigning it to whoever should follow up, tracking whether they've been contacted yet, and getting the information into whatever format you actually want to use — a spreadsheet, a printed list, or an email.

Logging in

Open the link at the top of this page. You'll see a screen asking for a 4-digit PIN — nothing else. No username, no password, no account to remember. Type in your PIN (see the chips above) and you're in — that's the whole login. The PIN alone tells the tool who you are, so it knows what name to show next to leads you touch and who "you" means when someone assigns something to you.

This isn't meant to be a bank-vault-level security system — it's an internal tool for a small team, and the PIN is just there to keep the "who did what" straight, not to lock out the world. Everyone who's supposed to have access already has a PIN.

Once you're in, the "Modern Line Furniture" logo in the top-left works like a home button — click it any time to jump back to the main Leads view, with any filter cleared, no matter where you are or what's open on screen.

Leads vs. Trade Shows

Right below the header are two tabs: Leads and Trade Shows. They're two separate lists tracked side by side in the same tool:

  • Leads is the main list — companies (reps, dealers, wholesalers, distributors, designers/architects/specifiers) who might carry, specify, or private-label Modern Line's furniture. This is the tab you land on by default, and where most of this manual focuses.
  • Trade Shows is a separate, shorter list of industry events — see Trade shows below for how it works.

Click either tab name to switch between them — nothing to scroll past, each one is its own self-contained view.

Finding leads

On the Leads tab is a panel called "Find new leads." This is the manual, hands-on way to search — you tell it exactly what you're looking for, and it goes and looks.

1
Category — 11 to choose from, plus "All categories" to run every one in a single go, scoped to whatever region you pick below. Every single one is the same underlying goal: a middleman who buys from Modern Line and invoices their own customer directly, under their own name — never a visible "Modern Line" branded line in someone's catalog, and never another manufacturer (a competitor, not a customer). The categories: manufacturer's reps, multi-brand wholesalers, hospitality dealers, foodservice distributors, designers/architects/specifiers/FF&E procurement agents/GCs, hotel management companies, restaurant chain & franchise development departments, hotel PIP (Property Improvement Plan) renovation contractors, casino/gaming FF&E procurement, senior living procurement firms, and event/trade-show/corporate furniture rental companies. Some of these (the designer/specifier-style ones) also cover the private-label angle — Modern Line manufacturing custom furniture that the firm then sells under their own brand, the way Harmony Contract Furniture does.
2
Region — type in a state, a region like "Pacific Northwest," or leave it blank for nationwide. There are also quick-click buttons below the search fields (Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, Tri-State, Southeast, Southwest, and so on) that fill this field in for you automatically, grouped the way furniture sales territories actually get talked about in the industry — not just individual states. Tri-State (NJ, PA, NY) is its own button separate from the broader Mid-Atlantic one, since that specific three-state combination comes up often enough on its own to be worth a one-click shortcut. Every search, regardless of region, only looks at the 48 contiguous states — Alaska and Hawaii are deliberately excluded, since shipping large furniture there isn't practical for this business.
3
Keyword (optional) — narrow it further, like "outdoor seating" or "banquette," if you want something specific.
4
Click Search. It's genuinely searching the live internet, not pulling from some pre-made list, so real research is happening while you wait — anywhere from 15 seconds for one category up to a couple of minutes for "All categories." When it's done, any new companies it found get added straight into your leads list, already fully verified (see Verifying contact info) with their best contact info already filled in. If a company it found is already in your list, it skips it — you'll never see true duplicates pile up.

Daily Scout — the one-click version

For when you don't want to fill out a form, you just want to know what's new
🔍 Daily Scout

This button, right above the search panel, is a shortcut for "just find me whatever's new today, everywhere." Instead of picking one category and one region, it runs through every category across the entire country automatically, one after another. It takes a few minutes since it's really doing five separate searches back to back, but you only have to click once.

When it's finished, it tells you how many genuinely new companies it found, automatically checks the boxes next to those specific ones (see "Selecting and exporting" below for what the checkboxes are for), and asks if you'd like to send just those new ones straight to a Google Sheet. Say yes, and you'll land right on a finished spreadsheet with today's finds already filled in.

Verifying contact info

Finding a company is one thing — knowing its phone number or email still actually work, and getting the best contact info available, is another. That's what verifying does: it re-checks a company's own website for the most specific, current contact it can find (an actual name and email if one's published, not just a general office line), and confirms the company and site are still active.

You don't need to trigger this yourself for anything found from now on — every new lead, from a manual search or Daily Scout, verifies itself automatically the moment it's found, before it ever shows up in your list. What you will sometimes need is "Verify all," the button next to Team in the header — it runs the same check on every lead in your list that hasn't been verified yet (for example, anything that was already in the list before this feature existed). While it's running, a small spinner and a live "3/47" counter appear next to the button so you can see it's actually working rather than frozen — it processes a few leads at a time and can take a few minutes for a large batch, but one company failing to verify (site down, etc.) never stops the rest.

Exporting also checks for you: if you try to export a batch that includes unverified leads, it'll ask whether to verify them first before the export goes out, so whatever lands in Excel or a Google Sheet has the freshest contact info available.

Once a lead is verified, its row shows a small "Verified" badge (or "Flagged" if the site or company couldn't be confirmed) with the date it was last checked.

A lead's detail card

Every lead in your list shows up as a row in a table, with columns for the company, category, territory, and so on. If a row feels too cramped to read comfortably, click directly on the company's name (not the small ↗ arrow next to it — that opens the company's actual website in a new tab). Clicking the name itself opens a clean card showing everything on file for that one lead: category, territory, why it's a good fit, contact info, current status, who it's assigned to, and any notes anyone's added. It's the same information as the row, just laid out to actually read comfortably.

One field on this card isn't just for reading: Category is a dropdown right there on the card, and picking a different one saves immediately — no separate save button, no confirmation dialog. Use this when a search puts a company in the wrong bucket (say, a manufacturer's rep that got tagged as a dealer) — fix it right on the card instead of needing to delete and re-add the lead.

Emailing one lead as a cheat sheet

Sometimes you want to hand a single lead off to someone — a teammate, yourself later, anyone — with enough context that they don't have to go dig through the tool first. Open a lead's detail card (above) and click "✉ Email this card." A small box pops up asking who to send it to and, if you want, a personal note (something like "call this one first, they seemed eager"). Click "Open in Gmail" and it builds a complete email — your note at the top if you added one, then the company name, website, category, territory, why it's worth reaching out, whatever contact info is on file, and current status — all formatted and ready. Nothing gets sent automatically; it opens the draft in Gmail and you review and hit Send yourself, same as writing any other email.

Emailing many leads at once

For reaching out to a batch of companies at the same time rather than one by one, check the boxes next to whichever leads you want (see Selecting and exporting for how the checkboxes work), then click "Email selected" in the toolbar above the leads table. It shows how many of your checked leads actually have an email on file (some only have a phone number — those get skipped, and it tells you how many), then lets you write one shared subject and message.

Clicking "Open in Gmail" opens a single draft with every one of those companies' emails in the Bcc field — meaning it's one message sent to the whole group, not a personalized note to each one individually, and none of the recipients can see each other's email addresses. It's meant for a general "here's who we are" outreach blast, not a substitute for the personalized cheat-sheet email above. As always, nothing sends until you review the draft in Gmail and hit Send yourself.

Assigning work to your team

Every lead row has a "Send to…" button. Click it, pick a teammate from the list (you can pick yourself too — it's marked "(you)" in the list), optionally add a short note explaining why ("they mentioned wanting a new supplier last week"), and that lead now shows an arrow and that person's name right on the row, so everyone can see who's supposed to be working it. This is the actual point of the tool being shared — instead of leads living in someone's personal notes, everyone sees the same list and the same assignments at the same time, from their own device.

The same "Send to…" pattern works identically for trade shows — see below.

Status, filters, and everyday row controls

A few more controls live right on the leads table that don't fit neatly under the sections above:

Status

Every lead has a status dropdown, right in its row: New (default, nothing's happened yet), Assigned (someone's been sent it via "Send to…"), Contacted (you've reached out), Responded (they got back to you), Partner (it worked — they're carrying the line), or Dead end (not a fit, don't pursue). Changing it takes effect immediately for everyone, and feeds both the stat numbers and the filter chips below.

Filter chips

Above the leads table is a row of chips — All, Assigned to me, New, Contacted, Responded, Partner, Needs follow-up — that narrow which rows are showing, based on each lead's status (or, for "Assigned to me," based on who it's assigned to). This only changes what you're looking at; it never changes or removes anything, and switching back to "All" brings everything back.

"Needs follow-up" is a little different from the rest — it's not a status you set by hand. The moment a lead's status is set to Contacted, the tool quietly stamps the date. If 5 days pass with it still sitting on Contacted (not moved to Responded, Partner, or Dead end), that lead shows a red "⚠ Nd, no response" note right under its status dropdown, and the "Needs follow-up" chip pulls up every lead in that state — so nothing you've reached out to just silently falls off your radar.

Refresh

The "Refresh" button next to the filter chips re-pulls the shared list from the server — useful if a teammate added or changed something on their own device and you want to see it without reloading the whole page.

The per-lead "Verify" button

Alongside "Send to…" on every row is a "Verify" button — the same check described in Verifying contact info, but for just that one lead. Since new leads already verify themselves automatically, you'd mainly use this to re-check one specific lead later — for example, right before finally making the call on something that's been sitting in the list a while, to make sure the number's still good.

Remove — one at a time, or in bulk

"Remove," on every lead and show row, deletes that one permanently — it asks "Remove this lead?" (or show) first, and there's no undo, so it's meant for genuine mistakes or entries that turned out to be irrelevant.

To clear out many at once — say you want to wipe everything and start fresh with new search settings — check the boxes next to the leads you want gone (same checkboxes used for export and mass-email) and click "Remove selected" in the toolbar. Leave nothing checked and it removes everything currently in the list instead — it'll tell you exactly how many before asking you to confirm, so you always know what you're about to lose.

+ Add lead manually

If you already know about a company — someone mentioned it, you saw it somewhere — and don't need the tool to search for it, "+ Add lead manually" opens a blank form to enter it directly: company, website, category, territory, why it's a fit, and contact info, all typed in by hand instead of found by search.

Stats row

At the top of the Leads tab, four numbers give an at-a-glance summary: Total leads, Assigned to me, Contacted, and Partners — the same counts the filter chips use, just visible without clicking anything.

Print / Export PDF

In the header, "Print / Export PDF" opens your browser's normal print dialog, but formatted for it — search panels, buttons, and checkboxes are hidden, leaving just a clean table of whichever tab you're currently on (Leads or Trade Shows) and its filter. From that print dialog you can print for real or save as a PDF, depending on what your browser and printer support.

Selecting and exporting

To the left of every lead's name is a small checkbox, and there's one at the very top of the column too — checking that top one selects every lead currently showing in your list. Checking individual boxes lets you pick out a handful of specific leads. This matters when you don't want to export or email everything — say you only want to hand someone the 5 leads from Daily Scout this morning, not everything you've accumulated over time.

ButtonWhat it does
Export to ExcelDownloads a spreadsheet file (CSV) straight to your computer, ready to open in Excel or Numbers.
Export to Google SheetCreates a brand-new, real Google Sheet — properly formatted with columns already sized to fit the content, no manual cleanup needed. It asks you to pick which Google account it should go into, so it can land wherever you want it (your Drive, a shared team Drive, whichever you choose in the moment).

Both respect your checkboxes: if anything's checked, only those leads get exported. If nothing's checked, it exports the whole list — nothing extra to remember, the tool just does the sensible thing either way. Both also check whether anything in the export is still unverified first — see Verifying contact info.

Trade shows

The Trade Shows tab tracks industry events where reps, dealers, and hospitality buyers actually meet manufacturers — exhibiting or just attending is its own lead-gen channel, separate from the company-by-company list on the Leads tab.

Finding shows works like the leads search — type an optional focus (like "restaurant" or "hospitality design") and click "Search for shows" to look for real, currently-scheduled events.

A show's detail card works exactly like a lead's: click a show's name to open a card with its dates, location, why it's relevant, current status, and who it's assigned to. Unlike the read-only notes on a lead's card, a show's card has an editable Notes box right on it — jot down anything worth remembering (booth cost, who to look for there, whether it's worth the travel) and click "Save notes." From that same card, "Send to…" assigns the show to yourself or a teammate, same as leads — this is how you'd flag "I want to look into this one" or hand a show off to someone else on the team.

Shows also have their own Export to Excel and Export to Google Sheet buttons, working the same way as the leads export.

Adding people to the tool

Click "Team" in the top-right of the page to see everyone who currently has access, and to add someone new. Fill in their name, pick a 4-digit PIN for them, and optionally their email. Once added, you'll see an "invite" box with two options: copy the invite text (a ready-to-paste message with the link and their PIN, for texting or pasting anywhere) or, if you gave them an email, email the invite directly through Gmail. Either way, all they need to get started is that PIN — nothing to install, no account to create.

Running it all automatically

A version of Daily Scout that runs by itself, with nobody touching a keyboard

Everything described above needs a person to open the tool and click something. There's also a fully automatic version — it runs on a schedule (once a day by default), searches for new leads on its own, saves them the same way a manual search would, adds them to a running Google Sheet automatically, and emails a summary to the team, all without anyone opening a browser.

Making something run completely unattended, with nobody around to click "yes" on a Google login screen or hit Send on an email, needs different tools than the one-click buttons above:

Why this needs extra setup

The manual "Export to Google Sheet" button works because a real person is sitting there to pick their Google account when asked. A scheduled job running at 8am with nobody watching can't do that — there's no one to click through a login screen. It needs a Google service account instead, which is essentially a permanent, non-human identity that can write to a specific spreadsheet on its own, indefinitely, without ever showing a login screen. Sending the summary email has the same problem — opening a Gmail draft and waiting for someone to click Send doesn't work if no one's there, so it uses Resend (an email-sending service) to actually deliver the message on its own.

Setting this up is a one-time, roughly 15-minute task, fully spelled out step by step in docs/DAILY-SCOUT-AUTOMATION-SETUP.md in this project's repository. In short: create a service account in Google Cloud, share one spreadsheet with it, sign up for Resend and get an API key, and make sure whoever should get the daily summary has their email address saved under "Team." Once that's handed off, it runs by itself from then on — no further setup, ever, unless something needs to move.

Once it's live, here's exactly what happens every morning: it searches all five categories nationwide (the same 48-state-only rule as manual search applies), quietly skips anything already in the list, saves whatever's genuinely new the same way a manual search does, adds those rows to the bottom of one ongoing "Daily Scout Log" spreadsheet (it only ever adds to it — it never erases or overwrites a previous day), and sends an email to your team listing each new company, its territory, and why it's worth a call.

Testing it on demand

Once configured, this exact same automated run can be triggered any time you want — no need to wait for the schedule, and it's a good way to confirm everything's working correctly right after setup:

curl -X POST https://igor-hq.techhausinc1.deno.net/mlf/scout/run-now \
  -H "X-Access-Code: modernline2026"

Changing the schedule from daily to weekly

By default it runs every single day. If once a week (say, every Monday morning) fits better, that's a one-line change in the code — a developer would find the line that says Deno.cron("mlf-daily-scout", "0 13 * * *", ...) and change the schedule part to run weekly instead of daily. Not something to do yourself — just flag it and it's a quick change.
